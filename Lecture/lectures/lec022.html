The RAG Chain

Retrieve the right passages, put them in the prompt, and require the model to answer only from them, with citations and an honest "I don't know".

Overview

A model alone knows nothing about Nimbus Coffee's refund policy, and if asked it will make one up. RAG fixes that by giving it the relevant facts at question time. It's the most widely deployed LLM pattern, because it works with private, changing data without retraining anything.

Concept

flowchart LR Q[Question] --> R[Retriever
top-k chunks] R --> F["format_docs
[1] (source: ...) text"] Q --> P[Grounding prompt] F --> P P --> M[Chat model] --> A["Answer with [n] citations"] R --> S[Sources for the UI]

The grounding prompt

You are the customer assistant of Nimbus Coffee Co.
Answer the question using ONLY the numbered context passages below.
Rules:
- Cite the passages you used with their number in square brackets, e.g. [1] or [2][3].
- If the answer is not in the context, reply exactly: I don't know based on the available documents.
- Never use outside knowledge. Be concise.

Context:
[1] (source: refund_policy.md)
You can return unopened coffee beans and equipment within **30 days** ...
[2] (source: shipping.md)
...

Each rule has a job: only limits hallucination, citations make answers verifiable, and the exact refusal sentence makes "I don't know" detectable by code.

The chain in LCEL

generate = (
    RunnableLambda(lambda x: {"context": format_docs(x["docs"]), "question": x["question"]})
    | RAG_PROMPT | llm | StrOutputParser()
)
rag_chain = (
    RunnableParallel(docs=retriever, question=RunnablePassthrough())   # retrieve + keep the question
    | RunnablePassthrough.assign(answer=generate)                       # add the answer
)
rag_chain.invoke("How long do I have to return unopened beans?")
# {"question": "...", "docs": [Document, ...], "answer": "You have 30 days ... [1]"}

Returning the docs as well as the answer lets the UI show sources, and lets you evaluate retrieval separately from generation.

Worked example: one question through the chain

Question: "How long does a refund take to reach my card?"

1. Retrieve. The retriever returns four chunks (k=4):

[1] refund_policy.md       598 chars   # Refund and Return Policy … ## Online orders …
[2] refund_policy.md       552 chars   ## Damaged or wrong items …
[3] loyalty_program.md     302 chars   ## Tiers …
[4] employee_handbook.pdf  343 chars   Training — New baristas complete the 3-week Barista Academy …

Only the first holds the answer. The third and fourth are there because k is 4 and something had to fill the places.

2. Format. format_docs turns them into 1,932 characters of numbered context:

[1] (source: refund_policy.md)
# Refund and Return Policy
…
Refunds are issued to the original payment method within **5–7 business days** after we receive the item.

[2] (source: refund_policy.md)
## Damaged or wrong items
…

3. Prompt. The context goes into {context} of the system message, under the three rules; the question goes into the human message, unchanged.

4. Generate.

Refunds are issued to your original payment method within **5–7 business days after Nimbus receives the returned item**. [1]

5. Map the citation back. cited_sources finds [1], looks up passage 1 and prints sources: refund_policy.md. Passages 2, 3 and 4 were in the prompt and are not cited: the model ignored the three irrelevant chunks, as the "ONLY" rule asks. The chain returns the question, all four documents and the answer, so a UI can show the sources and an evaluation can score retrieval on its own.

The same chain on an out-of-scope question, "What is the capital of Peru?":

[1] employee_handbook.pdf   [2] products.md   [3] company_faq.md   [4] careers.html      (1,410 characters of context)
A: I don't know based on the available documents.
   sources: none

Retrieval did not fail here. A vector store always returns its k nearest chunks, however far away they are, so the prompt was full of coffee facts. The model knows the capital of Peru and still refused, with the exact sentence, because the prompt told it what to do when the context does not contain the answer. Take that rule out and it answers "Lima".

The Project

A question-answering CLI over the Nimbus knowledge base that prints each answer with its cited source files, and refuses out-of-scope questions with the exact phrase.

Goal

  1. Write a grounding prompt with citation and refusal rules.
  2. Build a RAG chain that returns the answer and its source documents.
  3. Map citations back to sources in code.
  4. Diagnose whether a bad answer is a retrieval or a generation problem.

Success Criteria

  • Policy questions are answered correctly with [n] citations
  • Sources are printed under each answer
  • "What is the capital of Peru?" gets the exact "I don't know" reply
  • All 7 checks pass

Walkthrough

  1. Read aicourse/rag.py: it has the loading, chunking and store code you wrote in Lectures 020–021.
  2. Write the prompt with {context} in the system message and {question} in the human message.
  3. When an answer is wrong, print result["docs"] first. Was the right chunk retrieved?

Stretch goals

Optional, for when the checks pass. Neither goal is needed for "complete", and the checks must still pass afterwards.

  1. Extend. Return the exact quoted sentence that supports each citation, and verify the quote in code. Done when: every citation prints its quote and verified, and the out-of-scope question has no citations.
  2. Connect (EXER007). Stream the answer token by token and print the sources at the end. Done when: the answer appears progressively, followed by its sources and the time to first token.

Reference code, for after you have tried: EXER022/stretch/

Common Pitfalls

The model "helpfully" answers from general knowledge

Capital of Peru? Lima. Correct, but not grounded. For a company assistant that's a bug: next time it'll "know" a refund policy you don't have.

Too many chunks

k=20 floods the prompt with noise and costs tokens. Start around k=4 and measure.

Missing source names in context

Without them the model can't cite and you can't debug. Always include metadata in format_docs.

Discussion

RAG or fine-tuning for company knowledge?

RAG for facts that change and must be cited. Fine-tuning for style, format or narrow skills. Fine-tuning doesn't reliably teach facts and can't be updated daily.

Should a customer bot ever say "I don't know"?

Yes, preferably with a next step ("contact support at …"). A wrong policy stated confidently costs more than an honest handoff.
