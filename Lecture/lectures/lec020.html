Loading and Chunking

RAG quality is decided before any model is called. Good answers need clean text, useful metadata, and chunks that each hold one complete idea.

Overview

The ingestion pipeline turns a folder of files into chunks ready to embed. It's unglamorous and it matters enormously: navigation menus in your HTML, page numbers lost from PDFs, or a chunk that cuts a policy in half all become wrong answers later. We'll use the Nimbus Coffee knowledge base in data/knowledge_base/.

The Document

Document(
    page_content="Full-time employees receive 15 days of paid vacation ...",
    metadata={"source": "employee_handbook.pdf", "page": 1},
)

Metadata travels with every chunk. It powers citations ("handbook, page 1"), filters ("only HR documents"), and debugging ("which file did that wrong answer come from?").

Why chunk?

  • Precision: one vector per topic. A whole-document vector averages all its topics into mush.
  • Context budget: you put the top-k chunks in the prompt, and small chunks leave room for more sources.
  • Citations: point to the exact passage.

Chunk size and overlap

SizeProsCons
Small (~200 chars)Precise matchesFragments lose context ("it costs ₱250": what does?)
Medium (~500–1000)Usually a good balance
Large (~1500+)Full contextBlurry vectors; fewer fit in the prompt; more irrelevant text

Overlap repeats the end of one chunk at the start of the next, so a sentence near a boundary isn't stranded. 10–20% of the chunk size is typical.

Recursive splitting

RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter tries the most meaningful separator first and only falls back to smaller ones when a piece is still too big:

separators=["\n## ", "\n# ", "\n\n", "\n", ". ", " ", ""]
#            sections, titles, paragraphs, lines, sentences, words, characters

Splitting on Markdown headings first keeps "## Damaged or wrong items" and its paragraph together.

Worked example: one document, three chunk sizes

refund_policy.md is 1,153 characters long, with four ## sections. Here is what the splitter makes of it at the three sizes the project compares.

chunk_size=200,  overlap=0    -> 10 chunks: 99, 16, 165, 73, 158, 79, 25, 184, 171, 166 characters
chunk_size=600,  overlap=100  ->  2 chunks: 598, 552
chunk_size=1500, overlap=200  ->  1 chunk : 1152

At 600 the two chunks are:

chunk 0 (598)  # Refund and Return Policy … ## Online orders … 30 days … 14 days … 5–7 business days
chunk 1 (552)  ## Damaged or wrong items … 7 days with a photo … ## In-store purchases … ## Subscriptions …

The cut fell exactly before ## Damaged or wrong items. That is the recursive splitter at work: the document is too big for one chunk, so it tries the first separator, "\n## ", and packs as many whole sections as fit. No sentence was cut, so the 100-character overlap had nothing to repair and the chunks share no text. Overlap only appears when the splitter has to cut inside a paragraph.

At 200 the first six chunks are:

'# Refund and Return Policy … wants every customer to be happy with their purchase.'
'## Online orders'                                                              ← 16 characters: a heading alone
'You can return unopened coffee beans and equipment within **30 days** of delivery\nfor a fu…'
'not satisfied with the taste; we refund 50% of the price as store credit.'     ← who? of what?
'To start a return, email returns@nimbuscoffee.example with your order number.\nWe send a pr…'
'original payment method within **5–7 business days** after we receive the item.'

Now ask: "How long does a refund take to reach my card?" At size 200 the sentence with the answer is the last chunk above. It contains "5–7 business days" and has lost the words "Refunds are issued to the", which went to the chunk before it. The model would receive a fragment that never says what takes 5–7 days. At size 600 the same sentence arrives with its whole section.

At 1,500 the whole policy is one chunk, and one vector has to stand for returns, damaged items, café purchases and subscriptions together. A question about cancelling a subscription now competes with every other topic in that vector, and the prompt receives all four sections to answer about one.

So for this document, 600 is not a magic number. It is simply the size at which a chunk is about as long as one or two of its sections. Look at your own documents' sections before you pick a size.

Goal

  1. Load Markdown, HTML and PDF into Documents with useful metadata.
  2. Clean HTML of non-content elements.
  3. Chunk with a recursive splitter and explain size and overlap trade-offs.

Success Criteria

  • 9 documents are loaded from 8 files
  • The HTML text contains no navigation, script or footer text
  • PDF chunks keep their page number
  • You can explain the trade-off between chunk sizes 200, 600 and 1500
  • All 7 checks pass (offline, no API key needed)

Walkthrough

  1. Open careers.html in a text editor and spot the noise: <nav>, <script>, <footer>.
  2. soup(["script", "style", "nav", "footer"]) finds all those tags; .decompose() removes each one.
  3. splitter.split_documents(docs) copies each document's metadata onto its chunks. Then add chunk_id.
  4. Print a few chunks at size 200 and read them. Would you understand them without context?

Stretch goals

Common Pitfalls

Tables and lists split mid-row

The shipping table may be cut across chunks. Structure-aware splitting, or keeping tables whole, helps.

Duplicate content

The same paragraph in five documents fills the top-k with copies. Deduplicate during ingestion.

Throwing metadata away

Without source and page you can't cite or debug. Add more if useful: title, section heading, date, access level.

Discussion

Is there a "best" chunk size?

No. It depends on the documents and the questions. Pick a sensible default (≈600 characters, 100 overlap), then measure retrieval quality (Lecture 024) for a few sizes.

What about documents that change?

Re-ingest only what changed. Content-based ids (Lecture 021) make re-ingestion safe; deleting chunks of removed documents needs the source in metadata.
